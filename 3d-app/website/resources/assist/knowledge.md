## What 3DEEZI is

3DEEZI is an AI sportswear designer. Teams, clubs, coaches, schools and brands design
custom sportswear in real-time 3D, with AI, and with their own artwork. Designs are
saved to the user's account, can be reopened and downloaded.

Sports: Rugby League, Rugby Union, Football, Basketball, Netball, AFL, Hockey, Training.
Garments: Jersey, Shorts, Training Tee, Singlet, Polo, Full Kit (jersey and shorts together).

## AI Powered Sportswear

[Start with AI](/create/ai): describe the kit you want (team name, colours, style, mood) and
optionally attach reference images. 3DEEZI creates four different concept designs with
previews; pick one and it opens in the 3D Studio, fully editable. Needs an account.

- Pictures in the brief: paste them straight into the brief box (Ctrl+V), drop them on it, or use Add image (several at once): logos, references or a design you already have, up to 4 (PNG, JPG or WebP; other pictures are turned into PNG). In the app, paste a picture into the brief from the keyboard's clipboard, or use the add button.
- Your own design in 3D: attach your design (for example a flat front and back snapshot) and describe how to present it, for example "Create a 3D version of my attached design. Make the front a hero image, slightly turned one way, with the back smaller behind it. Create a nice background." (the "My design in 3D" chip writes this). With "Create an HD presentation mockup" ticked, the mockup keeps your design as it is (colours, pattern, logos and text) and follows the layout, angle and background you ask for; your own brand gets no 3DEEZI label. The four concepts below it start from your design too.

- Image editor: every AI picture can be edited: the Edit chip on AI concepts, Edit image under the HD mockup, in the Studio's Photoreal showcase and on Convert, and the pen icon in Assets (in the app: Edit on concepts, the mockup and showcases, and Edit image in Assets). Markup draws or circles where to change, Comment adds numbered notes, Erase brushes a part out (make it see-through or let the AI fill it), Remove BG removes a plain background, and Resize crops to square, portrait or landscape or extends the picture with AI. Type the change in Describe edits and press Edit with AI: each change makes a new version, the arrows step between versions, Download saves any version, Use puts it back, and Esc closes without changes.

## 3D Studio tools (left rail)

- AI ("Ask 3DEEZI"): type a change in plain words, e.g. "make it navy and gold with chevrons", and the kit updates. You can attach an image to match its style. Also: Photoreal showcase images.
- Garment: switch between Jersey, Shorts, Training Tee, Singlet, Polo and Full Kit.
- Colours: Main, Secondary and Accent colours with a colour wheel and eyedropper, ready-made palettes, and a two-colour gradient fill with an angle.
- Patterns: built-in patterns (for example Chevron, Stripes, Hoops, Velocity) for the whole garment or one area (chest and body, shoulders, sleeves, side panels, hem). Drag a pattern onto the garment to apply it to that area. AI print from words creates a custom pattern. Pattern coverage slider: shoulders only up to full body.
- My design picture (Artwork panel, My design picture): upload a finished picture of your design — the front and back of the garment drawn flat, side by side on a plain background (PNG, JPG, WebP, PDF or SVG). After a short check (which picture is the front and which the back, and which garment it is), 3DEEZI puts it on the 3D kit exactly as drawn: the whole pattern, your logos in their places, round crests stay round, and the 3D singlet takes the drawing's straps and armholes. In the AI designer, attach your picture and press "Put my design on the 3D kit" on the results. It is saved with the project and works in the app too (Artwork tool). It works for singlets, jerseys, training tees and polos: 3DEEZI recognises the garment from the drawing (sleeves or not) and you can change it on the check screen; on Convert, press "Put my design on the 3D kit" after uploading a picture. Photo-style mockups (for example the AI HD mockup) also work: the photo's lighting and folds are printed onto the kit, so a flat drawing gives the cleanest result. If a picture has no readable garment, the check screen says so and you can set Front / Back / Not used on each shape.
- Factory print layout (Artwork panel, Factory print layout, Upload print layout): upload the finished print file from the factory (PDF, SVG, PNG or JPG with the garment's cut panels laid flat, named FRONT BODY, BACK BODY, LEFT SLEEVE, RIGHT SLEEVE, NECK TRIM, LEFT LEG SHORTS, RIGHT LEG SHORTS). 3DEEZI finds each panel and its name, shows them on a check screen over the 3D view (change any name there, untick the hem fold box if the plain strip along the bottom of the body panels is a real band), then Put on the 3D kit puts each panel on a raglan training tee and shorts, exactly and the same way every time. Remove print layout goes back to the designed kit (Undo brings it back). PDFs are named automatically from their labels; pictures and SVG files are named by hand on the check screen. Sign in first: the file is kept in your saved assets (Assets, Print layouts tab, with a download button), and Save keeps the print layout with the project, so opening the project again (here, on another computer or in the app) puts it back on the kit by itself, named as you checked it. The stage label above the kit then reads Print layout and the file name. A print layout brings its own garment (a raglan tee, shorts, or both, from its panels), so the Garment choice does not change the kit while it is on. If the file is deleted from Assets, the project opens without it and says so: remove the print layout in Artwork or upload it again before saving. Administrators see the print layout on the order's kit page, with a button to download the factory file. Files up to 30 MB. In the phone app: Studio, the Artwork chip above the 3D view, Factory print layout (Upload print layout or Remove print layout); the same check screen opens in the 3D view. On the check screen, "Join the sleeve bands to the sleeves" (ticked) leaves out a white line printed between a sleeve's band and the sleeve, as in the mock-ups; untick it to keep the line. The layout is shown on a loose training tee (it hangs straight with soft folds) and training shorts (short loose legs, side splits with the hem band curving up into them, a gathered elastic waistband with three channels and a drawstring). With a print layout on, the Garment buttons choose what shows: Shorts, a top, or Full Kit. The pocket marks on the leg pieces show as pocket openings on the front of the shorts, and the tee and shorts fold as in the mock-ups.
- Artwork: add logos and images from your asset library or upload new ones, at set places (left / right / centre chest, full front, sleeves, back neck, back centre, shorts legs). Drag artwork onto the 3D garment to place it. Layers can be moved, resized, rotated, duplicated, reordered and deleted. A logo on the shirt's chest also appears on the shorts leg in a Full Kit.

- Numbers: player number and player name on the back.

- Garment column (far right): Full Kit, Jersey, Shorts, Training Tee, Singlet, Polo.
- Top bar: Project name box; Artboard (opens the Artboard to make artwork); 2D panels (switches to the flat front and back print panels, where added artwork and the fixed wordmark, crest, sponsor, name and number can be dragged, resized with a corner, turned with the top handle or removed with the red x; scroll or pinch to zoom, or use the zoom buttons and Fit in the bar under the panels; drag empty space to move around; double-click or double-tap empty space to fit; Reset layout in that bar puts the wordmark, crest, sponsor, name and number back where they were and shows any you removed; click again to return to 3D). ## 3DEEZI Artboard (vector editor)

The Artboard is a professional vector editor modelled on CorelDRAW, with 3DEEZI branding.
Everything drawn is a real vector that can be node-edited. Open it from the home page or
[here](/artboard). Page sizes from A5 to A1 (portrait or landscape), 500 x 400 mm, 300 x 300 mm, a 120 x 120 mm chest logo or a custom size; units mm, cm, inches or pixels.
Layout: menus along the top (File, Edit, View, Layout, Object, Effects, Table, Text, Help), the
property bar at the top of the screen just under the menus, which changes with the selected tool
(tool and effect settings and their Apply buttons are there, not in the right panel), the toolbox on the left, Colour / Properties / Layers / Assets tabs on the right,
a colour palette at the bottom (click a colour: fill, right-click: outline, drag a colour onto
an object; a saved palette you picked in the Colour tab shows first, then the Document colours), and a status bar (page size, units, grid, snap, zoom).
Right after the menus at the top are three quick-access buttons (cyan-to-violet, they light up when you point at them): AI opens AI Powered Sportswear, DYO opens Design Your Own (choose a sport and garment, then the 3D Studio) and HOME opens the 3DEEZI home page (in the app, its home screen). The artwork is saved on this device first and is still there when you come back to the Artboard. On narrower screens the buttons get smaller and HOME shows only its house icon; on the smallest phones HOME is left out (use the website's logo or the app's back button). Help > Tools and shortcuts On phones and narrow windows the Artboard has no menu bar (File … Help are hidden), so the AI and DYO tabs sit right after the 3DEEZI logo at the top left. Phones and tablets have no right-click: tap the ⋯ button at the right end of the colour palette (it lights up and a tooltip explains it), then tap an item: an object on the page, a page tab, a tool or a colour. Tap ⋯ again to cancel. A right-click (or the ⋯ button) on an empty part of the page opens the page menu on computers too. ### Object IDs

Every item on the Artboard has a short ID, numbered in the order items were made: #1, #2, #3 … Select an item and the status bar at the bottom shows "ID: #12" (point at it to see the item's name; several selected: "IDs: #3 #7"); click it to copy the ID (it says "Copied ID #12"). Use it in 3DEEZI ASSIST to say exactly which item you mean, for example "make #12 red" or "give #7 a tapered outline". An item keeps its ID when you add an effect to it or convert it; a copy (duplicate or paste) gets the next ID; a group takes the ID of the first item in it. Colouring a picture (palette, chat or eyedropper) tints it in that colour, keeping its light and shade; Undo restores it.

### Artboard tools, one by one

Select and shape:
- Pick (V): click to select, drag to move, drag the corner and side handles to resize, drag the round handle above the selection to rotate (or type the angle under Properties > Rotation). Shift+click adds to the selection; drag a box to select several.
- Free Transform (button 1, Pick flyout): select items first, then press where the pivot point should be and drag. On the property bar choose Rotate (turns them round the pivot by the drag's angle), Reflect (mirrors them across the line you drag), Scale (resizes them from the pivot, as if pulling their far corner) or Skew (slants them along the drag). Shift keeps 15° steps or even scaling. Tick Apply to duplicate to transform a copy and keep the original (handy for mirrored left and right designs).
- Freehand Pick (button 1, Pick flyout): draw a loop round the objects you want; everything whose centre is inside the loop is selected, and you are back on the Pick tool to move or change them. Handy for picking a few objects out of a busy design where a box would catch too much.
- Shape (F10): edits the nodes of a curve. Click a curve to see its nodes; drag nodes, handles or segments. Double-click a curve to add a node, double-click a node to delete it. Drag a box to select nodes. The property bar has: add / delete node, join two end nodes, break curve, close curve, convert to line or to curve, cusp / smooth / symmetrical node, reverse direction, extract subpath, select all nodes. Shapes like rectangles become editable curves first (Convert to curves, Ctrl+Q).
- Smooth (Shape flyout): drag along the outline of the selected objects to smooth out bumps and corners.
- Smear (button 4, Shape flyout): select an item first (with Pick), then choose Smear and drag across its outline: the outline is pushed out or pulled in along your stroke, softly, like pushing wet paint. Good for flame tips, drips and organic bumps. The brush size is Nib size on the property bar.
- Twirl (button 4, Shape flyout): select an item first (with Pick), then choose Twirl and hold the mouse down on its outline: the outline swirls around the brush, more the longer you hold. Direction (Clockwise or Counter-clockwise) and Nib size are on the property bar. Good for swirls, waves and flame curls.
- Smudge (button 4, Shape flyout): select a shape (or not: it works on the shape under the stroke), then drag from inside it outwards to pull its outline out along your stroke (like smudging paint out), or drag from outside inwards to press a dent into it. The stroke is the nib wide and narrows towards its end; the shape becomes a curve.
- Roughen (Shape flyout): drag across the outline of the selected objects to make it rough and jagged (a torn or brush edge).
- Attract and Repel (Shape flyout): hold and drag near the selected objects to pull their nodes in (Attract) or push them away (Repel), for organic bulges and dents.
- The shaping brushes (Smooth, Smear, Twirl, Attract, Repel, Roughen) work on the selected shapes and text, also text with a contour or block shadow (the text and its effect bend together and stay one item). Select the item first with Pick; picking a brush with nothing selected shows a reminder. They do not work on images (a picture has no outline): trace the image to vectors first (select it, Properties tab > Trace bitmap), then use the brush on the result. If a stroke changes nothing, put the brush circle over the edge of the shape or letters, or make Nib size bigger. Each stroke is one undo step.
- Smudge and Eraser work out each stroke in the background, so the Artboard never freezes: if a stroke would take too long (a very complex shape), it is stopped with a message and nothing changes; use a shorter stroke, a smaller Nib size, or simplify the shape first (Object > Simplify). Slow strokes work as well as quick ones.

Cut and erase:
- Crop: drag a box over the area you want to keep. Vector shapes outside the box are removed; if an image is selected, the image is cropped to the box. Crop works on what is on the page, so use it to trim artwork or cut an image down to the part you need.
- Knife (Crop flyout): drag a straight line across objects to cut them in two along that line (the selected objects, or everything the line crosses).
- Virtual Segment Delete (button 5, Crop flyout): point at a line or outline: the part between two crossings (where it crosses itself or another object) turns red; click to delete just that part. A closed shape becomes an open line (no fill, an outline). Good for cleaning up overlapping lines in a logo or a hand-drawn design.
- Eraser (X): drag over objects to rub parts out. Shapes are cut away along the eraser's path and images lose the pixels you drag over (the selected objects, or anything under the eraser).

View:
- Zoom (Z, button 6): click to zoom in, Alt+click to zoom out. Also Ctrl+mouse wheel, the zoom box in the status bar, and View > Fit page.
- Pan (H, button 2, right below Pick): drag to move around the page (it moves the view, not the artwork). Also Space+drag or the middle mouse button.

Lines and curves:
- Freehand (F5): drag to draw a line that follows the mouse, like a pencil.
- 2-Point Line: drag (or click start, click end) for a straight line; Shift keeps 15° steps.
- Bézier: click for a corner node, click and drag for a smooth curved node; double-click or Enter to finish, click the first node to close the shape.
- Pen: draws curves node by node like Bézier: click for corners, click and drag for smooth nodes, click the first node to close, double-click or Enter to finish.
- B-Spline: click control points and a smooth curve is pulled towards them (the curve does not pass through them); double-click or Enter to finish. Good for flowing swooshes.
- Polyline: click to add points joined by straight lines; double-click or Enter to finish.
- 3-Point Curve: drag from the start to the end point, release, then move the mouse to bend the curve and click.
- Artistic Media (button 3, right below Pan): draw freehand and the stroke becomes a filled shape in the fill colour. Choose the mode on the property bar: Preset (a shape along the stroke: Tapered, Teardrop, Flare, Wedge, Round, Wave, Pinched), Brush (textured: Dry brush, Ribbon, Chalk, Ink splatter), Sprayer (objects scattered along the stroke: Stars, Dots, Sparkles, Hearts, Leaves, Lightning, Confetti; with Spacing, Size ± and Random turn, Follow the stroke or Upright), Calligraphic (a flat pen nib: Nib angle) or Expression (with a pen, pressing harder makes it wider; with a mouse, drawing faster makes it thinner). Width (Size for the Sprayer) and Smoothing set the stroke. With a stroke selected, changing any of these redraws it, so you can try styles on a stroke you like. The result is an ordinary shape (the Sprayer makes a group), so colours, effects and Send to 3D work on it. Great for flame tails, swooshes, brush lettering, confetti and sparkle trails on kits.
- Painterly Brush (button 3, Artistic Media flyout): paint realistic brush strokes in the fill colour: Oil (streaky bristles), Watercolour (soft layered washes), Charcoal (grainy), Airbrush (soft spray) or Marker (even, see-through). Brush, Size and Flow are on the property bar. The stroke is a picture (bitmap), as in CorelDRAW; with it selected, a new brush, size, flow or fill colour repaints it (after erasing part of it, it stays as it is).
- Variable Outline (button 3, Artistic Media flyout): an outline that changes width along the line, like a brush or pen stroke (CorelDRAW's Variable Outline). Press on the outline (the edge) of a shape or line and drag away from it to make it wider there, or towards it to make it thinner. The first drag changes the width round that spot only (two points at the normal width appear either side); each press on another part of the outline adds a width point, and the width runs smoothly between the points. Drag a point (or the ends of its bar) to change it; double-click a point, or pick it and press Delete or Delete point, to remove it. The property bar has Profile (Uniform, Taper both ends, Taper at the start, Taper at the end, Bulge, Pinch, Wave), Width (the picked point's width, or the whole outline's width: all points change in proportion), Ends (Round or Flat, for open lines) and Remove (back to an ordinary outline). The object keeps its shape and fill; the outline is a shape of its own grouped with it, so it exports to SVG and PDF exactly as it looks, grows with the object when you resize it, and its colour is the Outline colour: palette right-click, or the Colour tab with Outline chosen. While the Variable Outline tool is on, the outline you are editing counts as selected, so colour and outline width changes go straight to it (no need to switch to Pick); Delete removes the picked width point, not the object. Pictures work too: click a picture with the tool and it gets an outline round its visible shape (a photo: its frame; a Painterly stroke or a cut-out logo: its silhouette), then drag on that outline as usual; Remove gives the plain picture back. Text needs Convert to curves first (Ctrl+Q). For a group (objects grouped or merged together), ungroup it first (Ctrl+U) to vary one part's outline; an outline colour given to the whole group still colours a variable outline inside it. Great for hand-drawn looking lettering outlines, swooshes that taper to a point, and calligraphic frames.
- Smart Drawing (button 7, Freehand flyout): sketch a shape freehand and it becomes the clean shape it looks like: a straight line (level or upright when nearly so), rectangle or square (also tilted), ellipse or circle (also tilted), triangle, diamond or polygon; anything else becomes a smooth curve. Shape recognition (None, Low, Medium, High) and Smoothing are on the property bar; the note at the top says what it became.
- LiveSketch (button 7, Freehand flyout): sketch with several quick strokes; when you pause (the Timer on the property bar, 1 s by default) they become one smooth curve. Start a stroke at the end of a LiveSketch curve to extend it (Join within sets how close, in screen pixels), or draw along part of it to reshape that part. Ends that meet make a closed, filled shape. Smoothing sets how smooth the curve is.
- Smart Fill: click inside an area enclosed by overlapping shapes to make that area a new filled object, without changing the original shapes. Pick the fill colour in the palette first (with nothing selected, a palette click sets the colour for new objects). Earlier Smart Fill results are ignored, so an area next to one can still be filled, and the same area can be filled again in a new colour. The area is worked out in the background: if the artwork round it is very detailed and it takes over 6 seconds, it stops with a message and nothing is added (the page keeps working); then pick a simpler area or simplify the shapes (Object > Simplify). Ctrl+A switches to the Pick tool, so after colouring everything pick Smart Fill again before clicking more areas.

Shapes:
- Rectangle (F6): drag to draw; Shift for a square. Corner radius (rounded corners) is in Properties.
- 3-Point Rectangle (button 8, Rectangle flyout): drag to draw the base line at any angle, release, then move the mouse to set the height and click. Makes a rectangle already tilted to match, for example, a slanted stripe.
- Ellipse (F7): drag to draw; Shift for a circle.
- 3-Point Ellipse (button 8, Rectangle flyout): drag to draw the ellipse's centre line at any angle, release, then move the mouse to set its width and click. Makes a tilted oval in one go.
- Polygon (Y): drag to draw a polygon; Shift for equal sides; set Points / sides on the property bar.
- Star (S) and Complex Star: drag to draw a star (Complex Star has crossing lines); set Points / sides and Sharpness on the property bar.
- Graph Paper (D): drag to draw a grid of rectangles (a group); set Columns and Rows on the property bar.
- Spiral (A): drag to draw a spiral; set Revolutions, Symmetrical or Logarithmic, and Expansion on the property bar.
- Impact (button 8, Rectangle flyout, after Spiral): drag a box to draw comic-style action lines. Style on the property bar: Radial (a burst of wedges pointing at an empty centre, for a dramatic background behind a logo or number) or Parallel (speed lines). Lines sets how many; Empty centre (Reach in for parallel) sets the size of the empty middle; Shuffle gives new random lengths. The colour is the fill colour, and changing the settings with an Impact selected redraws it.
- Basic Shapes, Arrow Shapes, Banner Shapes, Callout Shapes and Sport Badges: pick a shape on the property bar, then drag on the page. Sport badges are ready-made crest and badge outlines.

Text and tables:
- Text (F8): click on the page and type. Property bar (with a text selected, or the Text tool on): font, size (mm), bold, italic, underline, alignment (left, centre, right, justify: each line fills the width, for paragraph text and text with several lines), letter spacing % (0 = normal; more spreads the letters out, less brings them closer) and line spacing % (100 = normal; for text with several lines). Properties: weight, letter spacing % and line spacing %. Your own fonts: open the font list on the property bar, click Upload fonts… and choose the .zip as downloaded (for example from Google Fonts or another font site: any folders inside, licence and other files are skipped), or one or several TTF, OTF or WOFF files (choose several for all the weights; a WOFF2 font: convert it to TTF first). Files are grouped by the family name inside each font, with every weight (Thin to Black) and real italics; copies of the same style are added once. When the files hold several families (Google's DM Sans zip also has DM Sans 18pt, 24pt and 36pt), a box asks which to add, with the main one ticked. The font is added at the top of the list under My fonts and given to the selected text; Bold and Italic use its own bold and italic files, the Properties tab Weight list shows every weight it has, and it works like the built-in fonts (Convert to curves, PDF export). Uploading the same family again replaces it. Uploaded fonts are kept on this device (in this browser, or in the app); the × next to one removes it (it asks first). A design that uses an uploaded font, opened on another device, says which font is missing: upload it there too. Outlines for names and numbers: the Outlines button on the text bar (right of the line spacing box; also in the Properties tab's text card, under letter and line spacing) sets Outline 1 (next to the letters) and Outline 2 (around it), each a width in mm and a colour; they sit behind the letters and the text stays editable (double-click to change the name or number); 0 mm = none; Remove the outlines takes them off. Right-click a palette colour on such a text: outline 1 takes that colour. Convert to curves, SVG and PDF keep the outlines. Text on a path: select a text and a curve or shape (Shift+click), then click Fit text to path on the property bar (or Text > Fit text to path); the property bar then shows Along (mm: where the text starts along the path), Distance (mm: lifts the text away from the path, minus brings it closer), which part of the letters sits on the path (letters, middle, tops or bottoms), a button to place it on the other side, Centre, and Straighten (takes it off the path). A text resized with its handles keeps its size when fitted. Text menu: Fonts, Fit text to path, Straighten text, Convert to curves (Ctrl+Q, makes the letters editable shapes). To change a text later, double-click it (two quick taps on a phone).
- Paragraph Text (button 9, Text flyout): drag a frame on the page, then type: the text wraps inside the frame, like a paragraph. Drag the frame's side handles to make it wider or narrower and the lines reflow. Use it for longer text such as a team motto or sponsor details; use the normal Text tool for names and numbers.
- Table: drag to draw a table; set rows and columns on the property bar and type each cell's text in Properties. Fill and Outline colour the cells and lines; text colour and font are in the Table card.
- Dimensions (button 11): Parallel and Horizontal or Vertical: drag between two points to add a measurement line with its length. Angular: click the corner of an angle, then a point on each side, to show the angle in degrees. Segment: point at a side of a shape or a line: it lights up with its length; click to add the measurement, set off from the shape. It measures straight from node to node, as in CorelDRAW: on a rounded corner or a curve (a circle has four curved sides) that is the straight distance between its ends, and a rounded rectangle's side is measured between the corners' start points. Text is not measured: convert it to curves first (Ctrl+Q). For sizes and angles on print layouts.
- 2-Leg Callout (button 11, Dimension flyout): click the point you want to label, click where the line should bend, click where the text goes, then type. The arrow line and text stay together as one item; double-click it to change the text.

Connectors:
- Connector tools (button 12, right after Dimension; flyout: Straight Line Connector, Right-Angle Connector, Rounded Right-Angle Connector, Anchor Editing): lines that join objects and stay attached when the objects move, turn or change size, as in CorelDRAW; for diagrams, size charts, team sheets and design notes. With a connector tool on, every object shows its anchor points as small diamonds (by default the middle of each side, on the outline). Drag from an anchor of one object to an anchor of another: the anchor under the pointer lights up. Dropping anywhere on an object attaches to its nearest anchor; dropping on the empty page leaves that end free, where you dropped it. Straight joins them directly; Right-Angle runs only horizontally and vertically, with bends; Rounded Right-Angle has rounded bends (Corner radius on the property bar). Arrowheads (None, At the end, At the start, At both ends) are on the property bar. The tool stays on, so you can draw several connectors in a row.
- To change a connector: with the Pick tool, click its line (connectors are picked on the line, not the empty space round it); the property bar shows Type, Corner radius (rounded) and Arrowheads. Colour and width are the usual outline settings. If you select a connector together with other objects and move them, it rejoins its anchors when you click away. Editing its nodes with the Shape tool turns it into a plain curve that no longer follows the objects.
- Anchor Editing (button 12, Connector flyout): drag an anchor (diamond) to move it on its object (connectors on it follow); double-click an object to add an anchor there; double-click an anchor, or click it (it turns pink) and press Delete or Delete anchor on the property bar, to remove it: connectors on it move to the nearest anchor left. Reset anchors gives the object its four default anchors again. To move a connector end to another object: click the connector's line to pick it (its ends show as purple dots), then drag an end onto an anchor of another object, or anywhere to leave it free. Free ends always show as purple dots and can be dragged straight away.
- The chat can join two objects: select them (click one, Shift+click the other), then ask, for example, "connect these with a rounded connector" (straight, right-angle or rounded).

Effects (Transparency has its own button, 15; Contour, Blend, Drop Shadow, Inner Shadow, Block Shadow, Extrude, Distort and Envelope share the flyout of button 16; select the object, pick the effect, set the options on the property bar at the top of the screen under the menus, then press Apply there):
- Transparency: makes objects see-through. Types: Uniform (the same amount everywhere), Linear (fade, from solid to clear at an angle) or Radial (solid in the middle, fading out to the edges). Amount % (0% = solid). Merge sets how the object mixes with what is under it, as in CorelDRAW: Normal, Multiply (darkens, like ink over ink), Screen (lightens), Overlay, Darken, Lighten, Colour burn, Colour dodge, Difference, Exclusion, Hue, Saturation, Colour, Luminosity.
- Contour: copies of the outline of a shape or text, Outside or Inside it, or To centre (steps inside until the shape is filled). With the Contour tool, drag from the object outwards for an outside contour or inwards for an inside one; the steps show while dragging, and letting go applies them. Settings on the property bar: Steps (not for To centre), Offset mm (the gap between steps), Corners (Miter, Round, Bevel), Fill and Outline colour of the last step (the steps blend to them from the object's own colours). Select a contour (Contour tool or Pick) and the bar shows its settings; changing any of them updates it at once. Apply again never stacks a second one; Remove gives the original back; Break apart (or Ctrl+K) leaves the object and each step as separate shapes. Scaling a contoured object keeps the offset. If an inside offset is too big, nothing is made and the message says so; open lines have no inside (use Outside); pictures must be traced first. Ideal for layered outlines around team names and numbers.
- Blend: select two shapes, set the Steps and Apply blend: it creates the in-between shapes that morph one into the other (shape and colour).
- Drop Shadow: a soft shadow behind objects. Settings on the property bar at the top: X and Y offset (mm; positive X = to the right, positive Y = down), Feather (softness), Opacity %, colour, then Apply. Remove takes it off.
- Inner Shadow (button 16, Contour flyout, after Drop Shadow): a soft shadow inside an object, along its edges, so it looks pressed into the page, embossed or stitched in. Select the object (shapes, text, groups and pictures all work), then on the property bar set X and Y (mm: which way the light comes from; positive X and Y put the shadow along the top and left inside edges, negative along the bottom and right), Feather (softness), Opacity %, colour and Merge (Multiply darkens the colours under it, Normal lays the shadow colour over them), then Apply. Apply again with new settings changes it; Remove takes it off. As in CorelDRAW the shadow is a picture kept inside the object's shape and grouped with it, so it moves and resizes with it and exports as it looks.
- Block Shadow: a solid, hard-edged shadow at a depth (X, Y mm) in one colour, the classic sports lettering look.
- Extrude: gives shapes or text 3D depth (X, Y mm) with a side colour.
- Distort: Zipper (zigzag edges: tooth size and number of teeth), Twister (turns the shape around its centre by an angle), Push and pull (puffs the shape out or pinches it in by an amount). Then Apply distortion.
- Envelope: bends shapes or text into a shape: Arch up, Arch down, Bridge, Valley, Bulge, Pinch, Wave (flag) or Taper, with an amount in mm. Arch up is the usual curved team name.
- Mesh Fill: click inside a shape or text to add a soft spot of the current colour (spot size and strength on the property bar); click again for more spots. Gives smooth multi-colour shading. Remove mesh colours clears it.

Colour and fills:
- Colour Eyedropper (I): click any colour on the page (shapes, text or images) to pick it, then click objects to fill them with it. Shift+click picks another colour; Esc stops.
- Attributes Eyedropper: click an object to copy its fill, outline and effects, then click other objects to give them the same look. Esc stops.
- Interactive Fill (G): click an object to fill it with the current colour or gradient.
- Colour tab: fill or outline, Uniform fill, Linear gradient, Radial gradient or Pattern fill (stripes, hoops, diagonal, dots, checks, chevron, with size and background colour); RGB and CMYK values; Palette (the 3DEEZI colours and your saved colours, or one of your saved palettes); recent colours; Colour harmony (at the bottom): colours that go with the colour in the picker, click one to use it: Analogous (5 neighbours on the colour wheel), Complementary (the colour and its opposite, each with a lighter or darker version), Triadic (3 colours evenly spaced around the colour wheel), Shades (dark to light; grey, black and white always show shades).
- Gradients (Linear gradient or Radial gradient in the Colour tab) can have any number of colours. The colour strip shows the gradient, with a small square under it for each colour (a grey help line under the strip says how to use them): click a square to pick it (cyan outline; the colour picker, hex, RGB and CMYK then change that colour), click the strip to add a colour there, drag a square sideways to move it, double-click a square or drag it down off the strip to remove it (a gradient keeps at least two colours). After clicking a square, Delete removes that colour (not the object) until you click outside the Colour tab; the arrow keys move it. Under the squares: Position % (where the picked colour sits) and Angle ° (a linear gradient's direction: 0 = top to bottom, 90 = left to right; type a number and press Enter); then three buttons: Reverse (first colour becomes last), Space evenly, Remove (the picked colour; greyed out when only two are left). Then the gradient chips: ready-made gradients (3DEEZI, Fire, Ocean, Sunset, Forest, Chrome, Gold, Rainbow) and your saved ones; + saves the current gradient as a chip (double-click or right-click a saved chip to remove it). Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo gradient changes; the object stays selected and the strip and Angle box show it as it is. Selecting an object with a gradient shows its gradient in the bar, ready to edit; selecting something else, or nothing, puts back the fill mode you had. Changes go on the selected objects (and on new objects); Undo reverses them. SVG and PDF exports keep every colour.
- Palettes (Colour tab, under the colour values): the list shows the 3DEEZI colours with the colours you saved with + ("3DEEZI + my colours"), or one of your saved palettes, such as a team's or brand's colours. New palette… makes one from the selected objects' colours (or the current colour when nothing is selected); New palette from this design takes every colour used on the page, gradient colours too. Type a name and press Enter or Save (the same name replaces that palette). The picked palette also shows at the start of the bottom colour palette, with its name. + adds the colour shown in the picker at the end (it lights up briefly; a colour already there is not added twice), right-click a colour to remove it, the pencil renames the palette and the bin deletes it (it asks “Delete the palette …?” with Delete and Cancel). Palettes are kept on this device (in this browser, or in the app) for every design.
- Outline pen (Properties tab, shapes and text): Width (mm), Style (solid, dashed, dotted), Colour, Line caps (square, round, extended: how open line ends look) and Corners (mitered = sharp, round, bevelled = cut off). Give the outline a width first, or caps and corners don't show.

Right panel tabs:
- Colour: fill or outline colour, gradients with any number of colours, saved palettes, pattern fills, RGB / CMYK values, recent colours and colour harmony (details under Colour and fills).
- Properties: settings of the selected object (below).
- Layers: every shape, text and image on the page, the top of the list drawn on top. Click a row to select it; each row has Bring forward, Send backward, Show / hide, Lock and Delete.
- Assets: upload logos or artwork (SVG comes in as editable vectors) or drop files onto the page; click an uploaded item, or drag it onto the page, to use it.

Property bar and Properties tab (two different places):
- The property bar (under the menus, above the page) shows the settings of the tool you are using, for example Contour's Outside / Inside, Steps, Offset, Colour and Apply contour. With the Pick tool and an object selected it shows: Fill and Outline colour (X = none), outline width, line style, Opacity %, align buttons, X, Y, W, H with keep proportions, mirror, to front / to back, group / ungroup, duplicate, lock and delete.
- The Properties tab (right panel) shows the settings of the selected object, whatever tool is active. What it shows depends on what is selected: Name and Rotation (°) for everything; for text: Font, Size (mm), Weight (Regular, Bold, Black), Letter spacing (%), Line spacing (%) and Convert to curves; for a rectangle: Corner radius (mm); for a table: Rows, Columns, text size, text colour, text font and the text of each cell; for shapes and text: the Outline pen card; for an image: Image adjustments, Background and Trace bitmap (below).
- So if someone asks about a tool's settings, they are on the property bar; options in the Properties tab belong to the selected object (for example, Grayscale and Trace bitmap appear because an image is selected, not because of the Contour tool).

Images (Properties tab with an image selected):
- Import Image (Ctrl+I), or drop image files onto the page, or the Assets tab (your uploaded logos and artwork; SVG comes in as editable vectors).
- Image adjustments: Brightness (-100 darker to +100 lighter), Contrast (-100 flatter to +100 punchier), Saturation (-100 grey to +100 more vivid), Blur (0 sharp to 100 very soft), Grayscale (black and white), Reset (puts all adjustments back to 0). The original picture is kept, so adjustments can be changed or reset at any time.
- Background > Remove plain background: makes the plain colour around the edges of the image transparent, so the page or shapes behind show through. It starts from the edges, so the same colour inside the logo is kept. It needs a plain, single-colour background; Undo restores it.
- Trace bitmap (to vectors): turns the image into editable vector shapes (sharp at any size, recolourable, node-editable).
  - Colours (2 to 16, default 8): how many colours the vectors use. A one-colour logo on white needs 2; most logos 4 to 8; more colours keep shading but make more shapes.
  - Detail (Low, Medium, High): Low makes fewer, smoother shapes and drops small specks; High follows the edges closely and keeps small details. Medium suits most logos.
  - Leave out the background colour (on by default): the background area touching the image's edges is not traced, so the vectors have no box behind them; white or background-coloured details inside the logo are kept.
  - Delete the original image (vectors take its place): on, the vectors replace the image in the same place; off, the vectors are placed next to the image so you can compare them. Undo brings the image back.
  - Trace to vectors: runs the trace. The result is a group of shapes: Ctrl+U ungroups it, the Shape tool (F10) edits the nodes, and the palette recolours each part.

Status bar (the bar along the bottom of the Artboard, left to right):
- Pages: a design can have several pages, all the same size (front, back, sleeves, a team sheet …). The page tabs are at the left of the status bar: click a tab to open that page, double-click it to rename it, right-click it for Open, Rename, Duplicate page, Insert page after and Delete page; + adds a page after the open one. Each page keeps its own objects; Undo, Save and the autosave cover every page; Export PDF (File > Export PDF, or Ctrl+E) asks first when there are several pages: This page only (just the page that is open) or All pages (one PDF page each, in page order); a one-page design exports straight away. - Snap (tick box): turns snapping on or off. What it snaps to is chosen in View > Snap to (grid, guidelines, objects, nodes, page); choosing one turns Snap on.
- Zoom: a zoom list (Fit, 25% to 400%), zoom out (-), a zoom slider, zoom in (+) and Fit page (the square icon at the far right).

Files: File > Import (PNG, JPG, WebP and SVG; for a CorelDRAW .cdr file the Artboard shows how to
export it from CorelDRAW as SVG or PNG first), Export PDF (vector, Ctrl+E), SVG, PNG artwork (transparent background)

File > Send to 3D Studio (or the button top right) places the artwork on the garment. File > Save to my account (Ctrl+S) keeps the whole design, every page, in your 3DEEZI account, and File > Open from my account (Ctrl+O) opens it again on the website or in the app; Save as makes a copy. File > Save as template keeps a layout to reuse, and File > New from template starts from it or from a starter (front chest crest, back name and number, sponsor band, round sleeve badge). The design on the page is also kept on this device and comes back after a reload. Send to 3D Studio saves the design first and asks where the artwork goes (the kit in the Studio, a new kit, or one of your projects) and where on the garment; in the Studio, Edit in Artboard on that artwork opens the design again, and sending it again updates it in place. Signed out, Send sends just the picture. Menu ids: templateNew, docOpen, docSave, docSaveAs, templateSave.

## Which tool should I use?

- Curved or arched team name: Text tool, then Effects > Envelope (Arch), or Text > Fit text to path on a curve.
- Outline around a name or number: Contour (outside), or a thick outline in the Outline pen settings.
- Trim an image or artwork to one area: Crop. Cut a shape in two: Knife. Rub out part of a shape or image: Eraser.
- Classic sports lettering with depth: Block Shadow or Extrude on the text.
- Pressed-in, embossed or stitched look on a patch, badge or name: Inner Shadow.
- Torn or rough edge: Roughen. Smooth a wobbly hand-drawn line: Smooth.
- Flame tips, drips or organic bumps on a shape: Smear or Smudge. Swirl part of an outline: Twirl.
- Comic burst or speed lines behind a logo or number: Impact.
- Clean shapes from a quick sketch: Smart Drawing. A smooth flowing outline from rough strokes: LiveSketch. Paint-like strokes (oil, watercolour, charcoal): Painterly Brush.
- An outline that thins to points or varies in width (hand-drawn or calligraphic look) on an existing shape or line: Variable Outline (profiles such as Taper both ends).
- Brush-style swoosh, flame tail or tapered stroke: Artistic Media (Preset or Brush). Calligraphy look: Artistic Media > Calligraphic. Sparkle or confetti trail: Artistic Media > Sprayer.
- Rotate round a chosen point, mirror across a slanted line, or make a mirrored copy: Free Transform (Apply to duplicate).
- Delete the part of a line between two crossings: Virtual Segment Delete.
- Measure a side of a shape: Segment dimension. Measure an angle: Angular dimension. Label a part with a pointer line and text: 2-Leg Callout.
- Lines that join boxes or shapes and follow them when they move (diagrams, size charts, notes): Connector tools (straight, right-angle or rounded); move or add the points they attach to with Anchor Editing.
- Tilted rectangle or oval in one go: 3-Point Rectangle or 3-Point Ellipse.
- Select a few objects out of a busy design: Freehand Pick (draw a loop round them).
- Longer text that wraps (motto, sponsor details): Paragraph Text.
- Zigzag or sawtooth edge: Distort > Zipper. Swirl: Distort > Twister.
- Fade a shape out: Transparency > Linear (fade).
- Sharp logo from a picture: Import the image, then bitmap trace to vectors.
- Remove a logo's background: select the image, background removal.
- Put a photo or pattern inside a shape: PowerClip.
- Attach one picture (or any object) onto another so they move together: place it, select both (click, Shift+click), then Group (Ctrl+G or Object > Group); Ctrl+U separates them. Blend is not for this: it makes in-between steps from one object to the other (a morph), which is why it asks for steps and mixes the colours.
- Join shapes into one: Weld. Cut one shape with another: Trim. Keep the overlap: Intersect.
- Copy colours from one object to another: Colour Eyedropper or Attributes Eyedropper.
- Badge or crest: Shapes (sport badges, banners) plus text, or draw with the Pen / Bézier tools.
- Put artwork on the kit: Send to 3D Studio from the Artboard, or drag artwork onto the garment in the Studio.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I start designing?
Choose AI Powered Sportswear to create a kit from a description, or Design Your Own to pick a sport and garment and build the kit yourself in the 3D Studio. [Start with AI](/create/ai) or [Design your own](/create/sport)

### Show me the Artboard
The Artboard is the vector editor for logos, names, numbers and graphics, with CorelDRAW-style tools. [Open the Artboard](/artboard)

### Which plan suits my team?
Clubs and schools usually choose Club (5 team seats, shared logo library, approvals). A single designer or coach suits Creator; brands and resellers suit Pro Studio. Rookie is free to try. [Compare plans](/pricing)

### Do I need an account?
You can try the Studio and the Artboard without one. To save designs, use Projects, Assets and the Dashboard, or use AI, sign in or create a free account (Sign in, top right).

### How do I save my design?
In the 3D Studio click Save (top right) and give the kit a name. It appears in [Projects](/projects); each save keeps a new version.

### How do I download my design?
In the 3D Studio open Export: 3D mockup, Front & back, print panels, the PDF design sheet or the design specification. In the Artboard use File > Export (PDF, SVG, PNG).

### How do I add my logo?
In the 3D Studio open Artwork, upload your logo or pick it from your library, and choose where it goes, or drag it onto the garment. Logos you upload are kept in [Assets](/assets).

### How do I change colours?
In the 3D Studio open Colours and pick Main, Secondary and Accent with the colour wheel, or click a ready-made palette.

### How do I add names and numbers?
Numbers tool in the 3D Studio: type the player number and name; they appear on the back. The chest wordmark is under Text.

### Can I turn a picture of a kit into 3D?
Yes. [Convert](/convert): upload a photo, sketch or mockup and 3DEEZI turns it into an editable 3D kit.

### Can I design shorts or a full kit?
Yes. Choose Shorts or Full Kit as the garment, or switch in the Studio under Garment.

### How do I send Artboard artwork to the 3D garment?
In the Artboard choose File > Send to 3D Studio. The artwork is placed on the garment, where you can move, resize and rotate it.

### Which file formats can I export?
Studio: PNG images, a PDF design sheet and a JSON design specification. Artboard: vector PDF, SVG and PNG.

### What does the Crop tool do in the Artboard?
Drag a box over the area you want to keep: vector shapes outside the box are removed, and a selected image is cropped to the box. To cut a shape in two use the Knife; to rub out part of it use the Eraser. [Open the Artboard](/artboard)

### What does the Contour tool do?
Contour adds copies of the outline of a shape or text, outside or inside it. Select the object, choose Outside or Inside, set Steps (how many outlines), Offset (the gap) and Colour on the property bar, then Apply contour. Great for layered outlines on names and numbers.

### How do I show the grid in the Artboard?
Tick **Grid** in the status bar at the bottom of the Artboard (or View > Grid on / off) to show the 5 mm gridlines. To make shapes line up with it, also tick **Snap** and choose View > Snap to grid. The grid is never exported.

### How do I change the page size in the Artboard?
Use the page size list in the status bar at the bottom: A5 to A1 (portrait or landscape), 500 x 400 mm, 300 x 300 mm, Chest logo 120 x 120 mm, or Custom size to type your own width and height. The units list next to it switches between mm, cm, inches and pixels.

### Can I import CorelDRAW files?
Not as .cdr files: export them from CorelDRAW first. In CorelDRAW choose File > Export (Ctrl+E) and set Save as type to SVG (shapes and text stay editable on the Artboard) or PNG (a picture; tick Transparent background for logos). Then use File > Import (Ctrl+I) on the Artboard. Choosing a .cdr file on the Artboard shows these steps with a button to pick the exported file. If text looks different, convert it to curves in CorelDRAW (Ctrl+Q) before exporting.

### Is there a mobile app?
3DEEZI is coming to the App Store and Google Play. The website also works on phones and tablets.

### How do I contact support?
Ask here in the chat for help with the website. To reach the 3DEEZI team about orders, quotes, your account or anything else, use the contact form on the [Support page](/support) (add your order reference, 3DZ-…, if it is about an order); they reply by email.
